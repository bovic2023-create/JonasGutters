You step back to the edge of the driveway to look at something on the roofline. What catches your eye first is not the gutters. It is the roof itself. From the ridge down, running in vertical bands between the shingles, the color has changed. Dark brown, almost black in some areas. Streaky and uneven, like the roof has been stained by weather. You have noticed it before but always assumed it was normal aging.
It is not aging. It is a living colony.
Gloeocapsa magma is a cyanobacteria that feeds on the limestone filler in asphalt shingles. Every manufacturer adds limestone as a weight-providing filler material, and Gloeocapsa treats that limestone as a nutrient source. The dark coloring you see from the driveway is the organism's protective dark pigment, produced to shield the colony from UV radiation. Beneath that pigment, the bacteria are consuming the shingle substrate and releasing spores that travel downslope with every rain, depositing into the gutters below.
This is why a gutter cleaning performed without treating the roof surface re-contaminates faster than it should. The source of the biological debris is on the roof. The gutters are downstream. Treating only the downstream problem leaves the source fully operational.

Roof and Gutter Cleaning: Treating the Source and the System<h2>
The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) is unambiguous on one point: do not use a pressure washer to clean asphalt shingles. High-pressure washing strips the protective granules from the shingle surface. Granules are what protect the underlying asphalt layer from UV degradation. Removing them accelerates shingle aging significantly. The approved method is soft washing: a diluted sodium hypochlorite solution applied at low pressure (40 to 80 PSI, similar to a standard garden hose), combined with a surfactant that helps the solution cling to the angled shingle surface long enough to kill the biological growth at cellular level.
After soft-wash treatment, the visible staining fades over subsequent rains as the dead growth weathers away. Zinc or copper strips installed near the ridge release metal ions with each rain, washing down the roof slope and inhibiting new algae and moss spore establishment. A roof treated with soft wash and equipped with a metal preventive strip maintains its clean appearance for three to five years before retreatment is needed.
The gutter cleaning that follows the roof treatment addresses the system downstream of the roof: clearing accumulated debris and biological material from the gutter channel, flushing each downspout section from the top with pressurized water to confirm unobstructed flow, and inspecting the hangers, miter sealant, and fascia condition during the service visit.

Downspout Extensions: Matching the Extension to the Situation<h2>
The corrugated flexible extension is the most commonly purchased downspout extension in home improvement stores. It is also the extension type most likely to fail within two to three years from UV degradation, debris accumulation in its corrugations, and displacement by lawn equipment. Rigid aluminum extensions provide the same above-ground discharge function with significantly greater durability and a consistent slope profile that flexible extensions cannot maintain on uneven ground.
Rain chains, known in Japan as kusari doi, are a decorative alternative to closed downspouts that guide water visibly down a series of linked cups or chains by surface tension and gravity. They are appropriate for garden settings in light-to-moderate rainfall and create a distinctive visual and auditory experience. They are not appropriate as primary drainage in high-intensity rainfall regions where the volume exceeds what surface tension can carry through the chain. Knowing when a rain chain is the right choice and when it is not is the starting point for any extension recommendation.

Seasonal Maintenance Programs: The Documented Record That Catches Problems Early<h2>
A twice-yearly gutter maintenance program covers the two seasonal debris events that fill gutters most significantly: the spring drop of tree seeds, catkins, and pollen, and the fall leaf drop. The timing of each visit matters as much as the cleaning itself. The fall visit performed too early, before the last significant leaf fall from the primary trees on the property, creates a clean gutter that fills again in the next two weeks. The fall visit performed after the final leaf drop and before the first hard freeze removes the full season's debris load before it can be trapped under ice.
Each maintenance visit documents what is found: the debris composition and its degree of decomposition, the condition of hangers at each attachment point, the sealant condition at miters and end caps, slope verification at multiple points, downspout flush to confirm flow, and fascia condition observed from the working ladder position. That documented record, accumulated over multiple visits, identifies which sections of the system are showing hanger fatigue or sealant failure before they separate from the house.

New Gutter Installation: Color, Hanger Systems, and Downspout Location Planning<h2>
The aluminum used in modern seamless gutters arrives at the job site as flat coil stock with the color already applied as a through-coat baked enamel. This is different from a surface coating: the enamel penetrates the surface layer of the aluminum during the baking process. A scratch in through-coated aluminum does not immediately expose bare metal the way a scratch in field-painted aluminum would. The color stock is available in 30 or more standard colors, allowing precise matching to fascia board, trim, soffit, or siding color rather than defaulting to white or brown.
Half-round gutters use a different hanger system from K-style gutters. Rather than a hidden clip that locks the gutter to the hanger and the fascia, half-round gutters rest in strap hangers (also called fascia brackets) that mount to the fascia board separately from the gutter. The gutter sits in the U-shaped support of the strap and can be lifted out for cleaning without removing any hardware. Downspout location planning before installation accounts for the exit path at each downspout: where the water goes after it reaches grade, whether landscaping, pedestrian paths, or foundation setback requirements affect the extension routing.
