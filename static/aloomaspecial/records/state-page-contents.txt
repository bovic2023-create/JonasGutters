Roof and Gutter Cleaning in #State: The ARMA-Approved Method<h2>
On #State homes with asphalt shingle roofs showing dark streaking, green moss patches, or lichen growth, the cleaning method matters as much as the cleaning itself. Pressure washing, which many #State homeowners consider as a first approach, strips the protective granules from the shingle surface. Those granules are the primary UV barrier for the asphalt layer beneath. Removing them with high-pressure water accelerates shingle degradation significantly, and the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) explicitly recommends against it for this reason.
The approved soft-wash method applies a sodium hypochlorite solution at low pressure (40 to 80 PSI) with a surfactant that increases dwell time on the sloped surface. The chemistry does the work. Algae, moss, and lichen are killed at the cellular level without physical abrasion of the shingle surface. On #State homes with heavy lichen growth, lichen rhizines (the root-like structures that bond lichen to the shingle surface) release their grip more slowly than algae or moss and may require multiple rainfalls to fully clear after treatment, which is normal and expected.
Zinc or copper strips installed below the ridge provide ongoing prevention: as rain washes down the #State roof surface, it picks up trace metal ions from the strips that are toxic to algae and moss spores, inhibiting new colony establishment on the treated surface.

Downspout Extensions in #State: Choosing What Lasts<h2>
The most common downspout extension in #State home improvement stores is the corrugated flexible extension. It is inexpensive, easy to attach, and positions water a few feet from the foundation. It is also likely to become brittle from UV exposure within two to three years, develop debris accumulation in its corrugations that reduces flow capacity, and be displaced from its intended position by lawn mowers and foot traffic. The corrugations that make it flexible also make it impossible to maintain a consistent slope on the uneven terrain around most #State foundations.
Rigid aluminum extensions provide above-ground discharge with a consistent, maintained slope, resistance to UV degradation, and compatibility with both the 2x3 and 3x4 downspout sizes standard in #State residential construction. For #State homeowners interested in a decorative approach at specific downspout locations, rain chains (kusari doi) are an option where rainfall intensity and volume allow them to function properly. Seasonal Maintenance in #State: Timing the Fall Visit Correctly<h2>
In most parts of #State, the fall gutter cleaning is the most important of the two annual visits. It is also the most commonly mistimed. A #State homeowner who schedules fall cleaning in early October, when the largest deciduous trees on the property still have 30 to 40 percent of their leaves, pays for a cleaning that leaves a significant debris load still on the trees. The gutters fill again over the following three to four weeks. A cleaning scheduled after the final significant leaf drop from the primary trees on the property, and before the first hard freeze that would trap debris under ice, removes the full season's load in a single visit.
Spring cleaning targets the secondary debris calendar in #State: maple and oak samaras, catkins from elms and birches, pollen accumulation from conifers, and early organic material from the post-winter leaf decomposition cycle. This debris arrives April through June in most #State regions and, if left in the gutter, decomposes into an organic layer that subsequent summer debris sticks to rather than washing through.

New Gutter Installation Across #State<h2>
New gutter installation for a #State home, whether on new construction or full system replacement, begins with three decisions that most contractors make by habit rather than by design: the color of the gutter, the profile (K-style or half-round), and the location of each downspout.
Through-coat baked enamel gutter stock is available in 30 or more colors across #State markets. The color is applied to the flat aluminum coil before the gutter is formed, so the color is part of the surface layer rather than a paint applied on top of it. Matching the gutter color to the fascia board color in a #State home creates a clean, recessive look where the gutter visually disappears against the roof edge. Contrasting the gutter against the fascia or siding creates a deliberate horizontal design element. Both are valid choices; the difference is intentional rather than defaulted.
Downspout locations on a #State home are planned relative to the exit path the water must take after leaving the downspout: are there landscaping beds the extension would need to cross? Is there a pedestrian walkway that an extension would create a tripping hazard across? Is the foundation setback distance achievable from the planned downspout position? Planning these answers before installation eliminates conflicts that are difficult and expensive to correct after the gutters are hung.
